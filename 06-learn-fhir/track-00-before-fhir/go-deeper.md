## Go deeper

**If you want to understand FHIR's foundations before moving on:**
- MITRE's "FHIR from 10,000 Feet" module, part of the open FHIR for Research curriculum. A short, clinically-grounded introduction to what FHIR is and why it exists, written for an audience that hasn't met the standard before. Pair it with the HL7 FHIR R4 Overview for the spec's own framing. [mitre.github.io/fhir-for-research/modules/fhir-from-10000-ft](https://mitre.github.io/fhir-for-research/modules/fhir-from-10000-ft) and [hl7.org/fhir/R4/overview.html](https://www.hl7.org/fhir/R4/overview.html).

**If you want to see the layered-standards landscape applied to current US policy:**
- The Office of the National Coordinator's Interoperability Standards Advisory (ISA). A maintained catalogue of which standards the federal government considers most appropriate for which clinical exchange purpose, grouped by interoperability need (lab results, problem lists, medications, social determinants, and so on). Reading the ISA is the fastest way to see the layered HL7 v2 + CDA + FHIR landscape this track sketches in its current US-policy form. [healthit.gov/isa](https://www.healthit.gov/isa)

**If you want to go significantly further on the HL7 v2 to CDA to FHIR history:**
- MITRE's "Brief History of FHIR" module covers the standards that came before FHIR and what each was built to solve, without condescension. After that, the HL7 FHIR specification itself is the next stop. [mitre.github.io/fhir-for-research/modules/fhir-history](https://mitre.github.io/fhir-for-research/modules/fhir-history) and [hl7.org/fhir/R4](https://www.hl7.org/fhir/R4/).
