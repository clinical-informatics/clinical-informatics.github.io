# Go deeper: Research bioinformatics infrastructure

**If you want to understand the institutional infrastructure better before moving on:**

- [The NIH Office of Data Science Strategy](https://datascience.nih.gov/) is the canonical reference for the NIH-wide data infrastructure (cloud platforms, data-sharing policies, the Common Fund Data Ecosystem). The strategic plan and the policy pages are the operational reference for the federal-side infrastructure.
- [The CTSA (Clinical and Translational Science Awards) program](https://ncats.nih.gov/ctsa) is the NIH-funded national network of academic medical center translational science institutes. The CTSA program funds the institutional bioinformatics infrastructure at most large US academic medical centers; the program's resources page is the right reference for what a clinical informaticist would find at a CTSA institution.

**If you want to see the public-dataset landscape:**

- [The NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra) and [dbGaP](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gap/) are the canonical NIH archives for public and controlled-access sequencing data respectively. Browsing the dbGaP study list for any clinical area of interest is the fastest way to see what kinds of clinical-genomics studies are available for secondary analysis.
- [The Genomic Data Commons (GDC) at the National Cancer Institute](https://gdc.cancer.gov/) hosts TCGA (The Cancer Genome Atlas) and several other large cancer-genomics datasets. The GDC data portal is the operational entry point for cancer-genomics secondary analysis.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [The All of Us Researcher Workbench](https://www.researchallofus.org/) is the cloud-hosted research environment for the All of Us Research Program data (currently approximately one million participants). Registering for access and walking through the cohort-builder tutorial is the fastest way to see modern population-genomics research infrastructure at scale.
- [The European Bioinformatics Institute (EMBL-EBI) and its data resources](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/) are the European counterpart to NCBI. The EMBL-EBI data-resources page covers the parallel European infrastructure (Ensembl for genome annotation, UniProt for proteins, ChEMBL for chemistry); reading it alongside the NCBI material is the right way to understand the international research-bioinformatics ecosystem.
