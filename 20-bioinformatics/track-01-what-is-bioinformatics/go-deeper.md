# Go deeper: What bioinformatics is

**If you want to understand the field's scope and history better before moving on:**

- [The National Human Genome Research Institute (NHGRI)](https://www.genome.gov/) is the canonical home for the field's history, the Human Genome Project archives, and the current US genomics research portfolio. The "About Genomics" pages are the right starting point for a non-specialist.
- [The EMBL-EBI Train Online catalog](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/training/) is the largest free-and-online collection of bioinformatics training material in the world. The "What is bioinformatics?" introductory module is a 30-minute orientation that complements this track.

**If you want to see the field's clinical-genomics arc:**

- [Collins and Varmus, "A New Initiative on Precision Medicine" (NEJM 2015)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMp1500523) is the foundational US-policy statement that launched the All of Us research program and reframed clinical genomics as precision medicine. The paper is short and is the right reference for the political-and-scientific context that shaped the past decade.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [Coursera's Genomic Data Science Specialization (Johns Hopkins)](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/genomic-data-science) is the most accessible structured online curriculum in bioinformatics for a clinical or research audience. The first course (Introduction to Genomic Technologies) is the right depth for a clinical informaticist who wants to internalize the field beyond the concept level this track operates at.
