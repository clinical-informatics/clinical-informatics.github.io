# Go deeper: Population genomics, equity, and the future

**If you want to understand the large biobanks better before moving on:**

- [The All of Us Research Program](https://allofus.nih.gov/) is the US national population-cohort study currently enrolling toward 1 million participants with broad consent for research use of EHR, biospecimen, survey, and wearable data. The "About" pages explain the program's design, the consent model, and the participant return-of-results practices.
- [The UK Biobank](https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/) is the comparable UK resource (approximately 500,000 participants with deep genomic, EHR, and biomarker data). The UK Biobank data showcase is the standard reference for what kinds of analyses are possible at population-cohort scale.

**If you want to see the equity discussion applied:**

- [Martin et al., "Clinical use of current polygenic risk scores may exacerbate health disparities" (Nature Genetics 2019)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41588-019-0379-x) is the load-bearing single reference for the equity question in polygenic risk scoring. The paper documents the systematically poorer performance of PRS in non-European ancestry populations and the historical European-ancestry over-representation in the discovery cohorts.
- [Sirugo, Williams, and Tishkoff, "The Missing Diversity in Human Genetic Studies" (Cell 2019)](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674\(19\)30231-4) is a comprehensive review of the ancestry-representation problem in human-genetics research. The paper documents the historical patterns and frames the structural-equity question for the next decade of genomic research.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [The eMERGE Network (Electronic Medical Records and Genomics)](https://emerge-network.org/) is the longstanding NIH consortium that connects EHR-based phenotyping with genomic data across multiple academic medical centers. The eMERGE publications page is the most concentrated single source of papers on operational clinical-genomics-in-the-EHR work.
- [The Polygenic Score Catalog (PGS Catalog)](https://www.pgscatalog.org/) is the centralized public archive of published polygenic risk scores. Browsing the PGS Catalog for any disease of interest is the fastest way to see the current state of PRS development across complex traits.
