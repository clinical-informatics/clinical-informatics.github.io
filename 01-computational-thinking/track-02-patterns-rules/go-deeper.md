## Go deeper

**To read about clinical pattern recognition and where it fails:**
- Jerome Groopman, *How Doctors Think* (2007). A book-length investigation of how clinicians reason and where they reliably go wrong. The chapter on the patient treated for years for an eating disorder before her celiac disease was recognized is the canonical example of a working clinical pattern that swallowed a non-matching patient. Reads as one long meditation on diagnostic edge cases.

**For a case library that maps to the five edge-case sources in the essay above:**
- AHRQ's *Patient Safety Network* (PSNet) at [psnet.ahrq.gov](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/). The "WebM&M" library is structured around the question this track teaches: what was the rule, who was the patient, and where did the rule's pattern fail. Free and continuously updated.

**To go significantly further:**
- Robert Wachter, *The Digital Doctor* (2015). A book-length investigation of what happens when clinical rules and CDS get deployed in actual hospitals. The chapter on the thirty-nine-fold septra overdose at UCSF is the canonical worked example of stacked rules and stacked alerts producing a catastrophic edge case that no individual rule designer anticipated. The book is also the cleanest available case study of the gap between a rule that exists and a rule that has been honestly catalogued.
