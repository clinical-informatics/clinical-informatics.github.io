## Go deeper

**The original DAS28 paper:**
- Prevoo ML, van 't Hof MA, Kuper HH, van Leeuwen MA, van de Putte LB, van Riel PL. *Modified disease activity scores that include twenty-eight-joint counts. Development and validation in a prospective longitudinal study of patients with rheumatoid arthritis.* Arthritis & Rheumatism, 1995; 38(1): 44-48. The paper that derived the four-input formula and the original cutoffs. Worth reading for the section on why the joint count was reduced from 44 or 66 down to 28; the abstraction argument is explicit. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7818567](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7818567/)

**The parsimony argument applied to DAS28 itself:**
- Aletaha D, Smolen JS. *The Simplified Disease Activity Index (SDAI) and Clinical Disease Activity Index (CDAI) to monitor patients in standard clinical care.* Best Practice & Research Clinical Rheumatology, 2007; 21(4): 663-675. The SDAI and CDAI are arithmetic-only simplifications of DAS28 (a literal sum, no square roots, no logs). They perform almost as well in clinical practice. A study in how much of an algorithm's complexity is doing real work versus how much is statistical theater. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17678829](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17678829/)

**The cutoffs questioned:**
- Fleischmann RM, van der Heijde D, Gardiner PV, Szumski A, Marshall L, Bananis E. *DAS28-CRP and DAS28-ESR cut-offs for high disease activity in rheumatoid arthritis are not interchangeable.* RMD Open, 2017; 3(1): e000382. The cleanest available case study of the point the essay makes: the formula gives you a number, but the cutoff that turns that number into a clinical category is a separate, contestable layer. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28377428](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28377428/)
