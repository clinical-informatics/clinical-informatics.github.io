CHIEF COMPLAINT
Bilateral hand and wrist pain with morning stiffness, 4 months.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS
Ms. Reyes is a 48-year-old accountant referred by her PCP for evaluation
of polyarticular joint pain. She reports symmetric pain and swelling of
the MCP and PIP joints bilaterally, starting in October 2021, gradually
worsening. Morning stiffness lasts approximately 90 minutes most days. She
denies psoriasis, IBD, recent rash, sicca symptoms, or Raynaud phenomenon.
No history of low back pain or enthesitis. No fevers. She has been taking
ibuprofen 400-600 mg up to 3 times daily with partial relief.

She works as an accountant and has been struggling to type during tax
season. No tobacco use. Occasional wine, 2-3 drinks/week. Family history
notable for a maternal aunt with "arthritis" of unspecified type.

PHYSICAL EXAM
Vitals: BP 124/78, HR 72, T 36.8C, weight 68 kg, BMI 24.6.
General: well-appearing, no acute distress.
Joints: tenderness and synovial swelling at bilateral MCP 2-3, PIP 2-3,
both wrists. No joint deformity. Grip strength reduced bilaterally.
Tender joint count 8, swollen joint count 6. No active synovitis at knees,
shoulders, ankles, or feet on this exam.
Skin: no rash, no psoriatic plaques, no nail changes.
Remainder of exam unremarkable.

ASSESSMENT
1. Inflammatory polyarthritis, suspect rheumatoid arthritis given symmetric
small joint involvement, prolonged morning stiffness, and gradual onset.
Differential includes psoriatic arthritis (no skin findings, low
probability), polyarticular gout (atypical pattern, will check uric acid),
and viral arthropathy (resolving timeline argues against).

PLAN
- Labs today: CRP, ESR, CBC with diff, comprehensive metabolic panel,
  rheumatoid factor, anti-CCP antibody, ANA, hepatitis B and C screening,
  uric acid, TSH.
- Hand x-rays bilaterally.
- Start naproxen 500 mg BID with food for symptomatic relief.
- Follow up in 2-3 weeks to review results and discuss DMARD initiation.
- Patient counseled on the chronic nature of inflammatory arthritis and
  the importance of early aggressive treatment if RA is confirmed.
