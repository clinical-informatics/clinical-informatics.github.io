## Go deeper

**If you want to understand the framework before moving on:**
- Stef van Buuren's free online textbook *Flexible Imputation of Missing Data*, specifically Chapter 1 ("Introduction") and Chapter 2 ("Multiple imputation"). The introductory chapters walk MCAR/MAR/MNAR with worked examples that are short, concrete, and don't require any statistical background beyond what's in this track. The full book is the canonical modern reference on multiple imputation; for this track, the first two chapters are the right dose. [stefvanbuuren.name/fimd](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/)

**If you want to see the framework applied in a clinical paper:**
- Sterne, White, Carlin, et al. "Multiple imputation for missing data in epidemiological and clinical research: potential and pitfalls." BMJ 2009;338:b2393. Free on PubMed Central. Practical, clinical, and direct. Walks the MCAR/MAR/MNAR distinction with examples from epidemiological cohorts, then translates "what should I do about it" into specific actions a clinical researcher can take. The most-cited practical tutorial in the field. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2714692](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2714692/)

**If you want to go significantly further:**
- The full van Buuren *Flexible Imputation of Missing Data* textbook (cited above), all chapters. Free, comprehensive, and worked through with R code (the principles are language-independent; the worked examples translate to Python's `sklearn.experimental.IterativeImputer` and `statsmodels`). Read after you've made imputation decisions you're not sure about and want to know what the literature would have said. [stefvanbuuren.name/fimd](https://stefvanbuuren.name/fimd/)
