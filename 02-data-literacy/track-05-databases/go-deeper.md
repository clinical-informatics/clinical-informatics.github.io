## Go deeper

**If you want to understand the relational model before moving on:**
- The official PostgreSQL tutorial, Part I. Free, comprehensive, and written for readers with no prior database background. The first chapters walk what a database is, what tables are, what types and constraints do, and what `CREATE TABLE` actually means. The "Advanced Features" section then introduces views, foreign keys, transactions, and the relational features that make the difference between a spreadsheet and a database concrete. PostgreSQL is the open-source database closest to the standards; everything you learn here transfers to Oracle, SQL Server, BigQuery, and Snowflake. [postgresql.org/docs/current/tutorial.html](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/tutorial.html)

**If you want to see relational schemas applied to clinical data:**
- Chapter 4, "The Common Data Model," of *The Book of OHDSI*. Free online textbook chapter. Walks the OMOP CDM tables (`person`, `visit_occurrence`, `condition_occurrence`, `drug_exposure`, `measurement`, `observation`) one by one. For each table you see the column list, the types, the foreign keys, and the standardized vocabulary attached to each coded column. This is what a research-grade clinical schema looks like, with the constraint reasoning fully written out. The contrast with a "raw EHR extract" (Tracks 01 through 04 territory) is the point of the read. [ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/CommonDataModel.html](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/CommonDataModel.html)

**If you want to go significantly further:**
- The full *Book of OHDSI*, plus the FHIR R4 specification at hl7.org/fhir/R4. The two together are the canonical references for the two clinical-schema flavors course 02 has been pointing at. *Book of OHDSI* covers the research-warehouse view (one standardized schema across institutions, optimized for cross-institutional analysis). The FHIR spec covers the interoperability view (resources designed to travel between systems, with explicit constraints and value sets). Reading both, side by side, is the fastest way to internalize that "clinical schema" is two distinct disciplines solving two distinct problems, both built on the relational thinking in this track. [ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/) and [hl7.org/fhir/R4](https://www.hl7.org/fhir/R4/).
