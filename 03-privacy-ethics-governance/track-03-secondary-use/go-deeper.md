## Go deeper

**If you want the institutional response to the Henrietta Lacks case:**
- The NIH-Lacks Family Agreement page on NIH's grants and policy site. Walks the structure of the HeLa Genome Data Access Working Group, the composition of the panel (two Lacks family members alongside bioethicists and geneticists), and the data-use terms. Read it as the working example of "what governance for a contested legacy dataset actually looks like sixty-two years after the fact." Free, official, kept current. [grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/dms/privacy/nih-lacks-family-agreement](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/sharing-policies/dms/privacy/nih-lacks-family-agreement)

**If you want a peer-reviewed analysis of the Project Nightingale case:**
- Schneble, Elger, and Shaw, "Google's Project Nightingale highlights the necessity of data science ethics review," EMBO Molecular Medicine, 2020. Free on PubMed Central. Short (about 1,800 words), pointed, and the clearest published analysis of why a HIPAA-compliant data-sharing partnership can nonetheless break trust at scale. The authors argue for institutional ethics review of large data-science projects independent of (and additional to) HIPAA compliance. Worth pairing with the Wikipedia entry for the full chronology and the cited HHS investigation findings. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7059004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7059004/)

**If you want to go significantly further on the philosophical foundations:**
- *The Belmont Report* (1979), from the HHS Office for Human Research Protections. Free, twenty-two pages, the foundational document for the US human-subjects research framework. The three principles (respect for persons, beneficence, justice) are the philosophical scaffolding behind every IRB review and behind the ethical analysis in this track. Pair with Beauchamp and Childress's "Principles of Biomedical Ethics" (the textbook treatment, paid) if you want the deeper academic framing; the Belmont Report alone gives you the working vocabulary. [hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/belmont-report](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/belmont-report/index.html)
