## Go deeper

**If you want to understand the foundational re-identification finding:**
- Latanya Sweeney, "Simple Demographics Often Identify People Uniquely," Carnegie Mellon Data Privacy Working Paper No. 3, 2000. The paper that produced the "87% of the United States population is uniquely identifiable by (ZIP + sex + DOB)" finding cited throughout this track. Short, readable, foundational. The Harvard Privacy Tools Project hosts the paper page with a free PDF download. [privacytools.seas.harvard.edu/publications/simple-demographics-often-identify-people-uniquely](https://privacytools.seas.harvard.edu/publications/simple-demographics-often-identify-people-uniquely)

**If you want to see re-identification carried out on a real public release:**
- Arvind Narayanan and Vitaly Shmatikov, "How To Break Anonymity of the Netflix Prize Dataset," arXiv 2008. The Netflix-Prize de-anonymization paper. Free on arXiv. Even at a skim, the introduction and the worked attack section give the clearest answer in the literature to "how can a dataset with no names possibly leak identity?" The high-dimensional sparse-data argument generalizes directly to clinical longitudinal data (a patient's sequence of diagnoses, procedures, and lab values is the medical analog of a sequence of obscure movie ratings). [arxiv.org/abs/cs/0610105](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0610105)

**If you want to go significantly further on the clinical-research-specific framing:**
- "Individual Privacy" chapter from the NIH Pragmatic Trials Collaboratory's *Rethinking Clinical Trials: A Living Textbook*. Free online. Covers how the threat model in this track translates into the regulatory and operational reality of running pragmatic clinical trials. Walks the privacy considerations that show up in trial design (consent for secondary use, data linkages across health systems, return of incidental findings) that the academic privacy literature usually doesn't talk about. Pair it with the *Privacy, Confidentiality, and Health Research* preview chapters from Cambridge if you want the deeper IRB-and-policy treatment. [rethinkingclinicaltrials.org/chapters/ethics-and-regulatory/special-privacy-considerations/individual-privacy](https://rethinkingclinicaltrials.org/chapters/ethics-and-regulatory/special-privacy-considerations/individual-privacy/)
