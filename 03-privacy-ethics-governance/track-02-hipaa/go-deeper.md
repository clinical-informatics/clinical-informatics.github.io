## Go deeper

**If you want the authoritative source on de-identification before moving on:**
- The HHS Office for Civil Rights "Guidance Regarding Methods for De-identification of Protected Health Information in Accordance with the HIPAA Privacy Rule." The official government walkthrough of Safe Harbor and Expert Determination, with worked examples and FAQs. Free, written for non-lawyers, the source every other write-up cites. Read the main landing page for the overview, then the 2012 PDF for the detailed worked examples (still the operative reference). [hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/de-identification](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/de-identification/index.html) and [hhs.gov/sites/default/files/ocr/privacy/hipaa/understanding/coveredentities/De-identification/hhs_deid_guidance.pdf](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/ocr/privacy/hipaa/understanding/coveredentities/De-identification/hhs_deid_guidance.pdf)

**If you want the federal floor for research ethics that sits underneath HIPAA:**
- *The Belmont Report* (1979), from the HHS Office for Human Research Protections. The foundational document for the US human-subjects research framework. Forty-five years old, twenty-two pages, still the conceptual backbone of every IRB review in the country. Three principles (respect for persons, beneficence, justice) that translate directly into the consent, risk-benefit, and equitable-selection requirements that show up in modern IRB protocols. Pair with the OHRP Common Rule page for the operational regulatory framework that implements those principles. [hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/belmont-report](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/belmont-report/index.html) and [hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/regulations/common-rule](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/regulations-and-policy/regulations/common-rule/index.html)

**If you want to go significantly further on the operational side:**
- The NIH Pragmatic Trials Collaboratory's *Rethinking Clinical Trials: A Living Textbook*, "Ethics & Regulatory" section. Free online, written by a working community of clinical trialists, kept current as guidance evolves. Walks the operational reality of running clinical research under HIPAA, the Common Rule, FDA regulations, and state laws, including the cases where they overlap, conflict, and leave gaps. The "informed consent in pragmatic trials" and "individual privacy" chapters are the cleanest treatments of the hardest practical questions in this space. [rethinkingclinicaltrials.org/chapters/ethics-and-regulatory](https://rethinkingclinicaltrials.org/chapters/ethics-and-regulatory/)
