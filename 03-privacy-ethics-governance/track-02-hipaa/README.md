# Track 02: HIPAA and beyond

HIPAA is the United States legal floor for clinical data privacy. It is not the ceiling, and it is not the whole story. This track covers what the floor requires, where it stops, and what fills the gap.

Track 01 built the threat model. Track 02 walks the regulatory response: what the federal government decided was the minimum acceptable handling, what alternative paths it left for research, and which datasets fall outside its reach.

Five pieces:

1. **What HIPAA covers.** The definition of Protected Health Information, who counts as a covered entity, and who counts as a business associate. The four pillars: Privacy Rule, Security Rule, Breach Notification Rule, Enforcement Rule.
2. **The two paths to de-identification.** Safe Harbor (remove these eighteen things) versus Expert Determination (a qualified expert certifies low risk). When each one is the right answer.
3. **Limited Data Sets and Data Use Agreements.** The middle path for research that needs more than Safe Harbor allows but less than a full identifiable dataset. What a DUA must contain, what it must restrict, what it cannot waive.
4. **IRB basics.** When IRB review is required for clinical-data research, what "exempt" actually means, and the difference between secondary-use research on existing data and prospective research with new data collection.
5. **Beyond HIPAA.** State laws (California's CCPA, Illinois's BIPA, Washington's My Health My Data Act), 42 CFR Part 2 (substance use disorder records), GDPR for international data, and the apps/wearables gap that HIPAA does not cover.

The interactive piece is a pathway selector. The learner describes a proposed use of clinical data through a series of dropdowns, and the app recommends one of: no PHI involved (no HIPAA path needed), Safe Harbor de-identification, Limited Data Set with DUA, Expert Determination, or fully identifiable data requiring IRB approval. Each recommendation includes a one-paragraph rationale and a list of conditions that still need to hold.
