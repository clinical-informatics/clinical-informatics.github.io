# Track 05: Governance structures

Decisions get made somewhere. The question is where, by whom, and whether you are in the room.

Tracks 01 through 04 built a working vocabulary for the privacy threat model, the legal floor (HIPAA, state law, GDPR), the ethical layer (secondary use), and the algorithmic-equity layer. None of those frameworks deploys itself. A person on some committee, on some Wednesday afternoon, has to approve the IRB protocol, sign the DUA, accept the vendor contract, and certify the deployment plan. This track is about that committee.

Five pieces:

1. **The map of governance bodies.** IRB. Privacy office. Compliance. Data governance committee. AI/ML governance committee. Vendor management. Clinical informatics steering. Security. Legal. Each has decision rights over a slice of any clinical-data project, and most projects need approval from several of them.
2. **Decision rights.** Who actually says yes or no, on what dimension. In most institutions the decision rights are unclear, and the project gets stuck or pushed through depending on who is paying attention.
3. **The clinician's role.** Most governance committees are staffed by administrators, lawyers, and IT professionals. Clinicians are often absent, and the absence shows in the decisions. Which committee should you ask a seat on, and what should you bring?
4. **Vendor contracts.** Most of the *actual policy* that governs a clinical AI deployment lives in the BAA, the master services agreement, the DUA, and the SLA. The committee approves the policy. The contract is the policy. Read the contracts.
5. **Patient and community representation.** Who speaks for the patients whose data is being used? Patient and Family Advisory Councils, community boards, IRB unaffiliated members. Most institutions have these on paper. The question is how meaningful the representation is in practice.

Two case studies anchor the track: **Project Nightingale** (governance failure: a major partnership was not disclosed or governed transparently) and **Duke Sepsis Watch** (governance done well: a deployed AI tool with built-in oversight, monitoring, and clinical leadership).

The interactive piece is a project routing tool. The learner describes a proposed clinical-data project across a few dimensions, and the app produces a list of which governance bodies should approve it, what each will ask, and where the missing decision rights probably are. The output is the agenda for the first conversation, not the decision.


**Prerequisites:** Tracks 01 through 04 of this course. The governance framework rides on top of the legal (Track 02), ethical (Track 03), and equity (Track 04) layers.
