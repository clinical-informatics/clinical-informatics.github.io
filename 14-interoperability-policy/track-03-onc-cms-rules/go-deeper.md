## Go deeper: ONC and CMS interoperability rules

**If you want the ONC-side rule explained by the agency itself:**

- [The ONC HTI-1 Final Rule page](https://www.healthit.gov/topic/laws-regulation-and-policy/health-data-technology-and-interoperability-certification-program) is the federal landing page for the 2024 rule that revises the Cures Act Final Rule, advances USCDI to v3 (since superseded by v4 in 2024, v5 in 2024, v6 in 2025), and introduces the Decision Support Intervention transparency requirements that govern AI and rule-based CDS in certified health IT. The page is the reference for the current state of the ONC-side mandates.

**If you want the CMS-side rule explained by the agency itself:**

- [The CMS Interoperability and Patient Access Final Rule (CMS-9115-F) page](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/burden-reduction/overview/interoperability/policies-regulations/cms-interoperability-patient-access-final-rule-cms-9115-f) is the federal landing page for the 2020 rule that requires CMS-regulated payers to operate the Patient Access API, Provider Directory API, and Payer-to-Payer exchange on FHIR R4 and USCDI. The 2024 follow-on rule (CMS-0057-F, Advancing Interoperability and Improving Prior Authorization) extends the framework to a Prior Authorization API and a Provider Access API; both rules are linked from the CMS interoperability landing pages.

**If you want to go significantly further into the data set that all of this carries:**

- [The ASTP/ONC USCDI standard page](https://isp.healthit.gov/united-states-core-data-interoperability-uscdi) lists every version of the United States Core Data for Interoperability and the data classes each version contains. Reading USCDI v1 alongside v6 (and the v7 draft) is the most efficient way to see how the regulatory floor for what an EHR has to exchange has expanded over time, including the addition of social determinants, encounter detail, clinical tests, and AI-output data classes.
