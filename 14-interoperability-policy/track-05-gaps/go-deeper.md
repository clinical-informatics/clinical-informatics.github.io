## Go deeper: Where the gaps still are

**If you want to see the TEFCA network up close:**

- [The TEFCA Recognized Coordinating Entity site](https://rce.sequoiaproject.org/), operated by The Sequoia Project under contract with ASTP/ONC, lists the currently designated Qualified Health Information Networks (eHealth Exchange, Epic Nexus, Health Gorilla, Kno2, MedAllies, KONZA, CommonWell, and others as designation continues), the Common Agreement that they all sign, and the live operational statistics for documents exchanged. It is the working example of a federally-coordinated nationwide network and the reference for the QHIN-to-QHIN exchange model.

**If you want the patient-matching problem framed for an operating health system:**

- [The Sequoia Project's "Framework for Cross-Organizational Patient Identity Management"](https://sequoiaproject.org/framework-for-cross-organizational-patient-identity-management-2/) is the working group's analysis of the U.S. patient-matching problem, including the maturity model for matching capability (five levels), the minimum acceptable practices for cross-organizational matching, and the case studies showing match rates improving from 10% to 95%+ when those practices are adopted. The document is the practical anchor for the standards-side response to the absence of a national patient identifier.

**If you want to track the USCDI gap directly:**

- [The ASTP/ONC USCDI standard page](https://isp.healthit.gov/united-states-core-data-interoperability-uscdi) lists every USCDI version and the data classes each contains. Comparing v1 (2020) with v6 (July 2025) and the v7 draft (January 2026) is the most efficient way to see which clinical data classes still lag (predictive-model output details, harmonized SDOH at population scale, longitudinal patient-reported outcomes) and which were closed in successive versions. The page is also where the rulemaking comment periods for the next USCDI revision are posted.
