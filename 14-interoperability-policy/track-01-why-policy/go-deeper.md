## Go deeper: Why policy exists

**If you want the term defined by the agency that enforces it:**

- [The ONC Information Blocking page](https://www.healthit.gov/topic/information-blocking) is the federal government's primary explainer. The page links to the statutory text at Cures Act Section 4004, the 45 CFR Part 171 regulatory text, the current list of exceptions, and the OIG enforcement materials. It is the reference an informaticist responding to a complaint or designing a process under the rule will keep open.

**If you want the empirical evidence the policy was responding to:**

- Adler-Milstein and Pfeifer's 2017 paper in The Milbank Quarterly, [Information Blocking: Is It Occurring and What Policy Strategies Can Address It?](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5339397/) (open access via PMC) is the survey of HIE leaders that documented the prevalence of blocking practices in the years before the Cures Act. The paper named the categories that the statutory definition would later codify and is the empirical reference behind the federal policy move.

**If you want to go significantly further into the legal landscape:**

- Mello, Adler-Milstein, Ding, and Savage's 2018 paper in The Milbank Quarterly, [Legal Barriers to the Growth of Health Information Exchange: Boulders or Pebbles?](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5835678/) (open access via PMC) is the comprehensive legal analysis of what HIPAA, state privacy law, the Cures Act, and the absent national patient identifier each contribute to. The paper sits across Tracks 01, 02, 04, and 05 of this course and remains a foundational reference.
