## Go deeper: 21st Century Cures Act

**If you want the agency-issued framing of what the Cures Act does:**

- [The ONC Cures Act Final Rule page](https://www.healthit.gov/curesrule/) is the federal government's plain-English landing page for the rule that implements the Cures Act's information-blocking and interoperability provisions. The page links to the statutory text, the regulatory text at 45 CFR Part 170 and 171, and the rolling sub-pages for each requirement and exception. It is the page to keep open when reading any commentary on the Act.

**If you want the exceptions catalogued one by one:**

- [The ONC Information Blocking page within the Cures Act Final Rule](https://www.healthit.gov/curesrule/final-rule-policy/information-blocking) is where the current list of exceptions lives (eight original plus the two added through HTI-1: a TEFCA Manner Exception and a Protecting Care Access Exception, current as of 2024). Each exception has its own sub-page describing the conditions a practice must meet to fall within the exception rather than the prohibition. This page is the operational reference for designing or evaluating a restriction.

**If you want to go significantly further into the legal and policy landscape:**

- Mello, Adler-Milstein, Ding, and Savage's 2018 paper in The Milbank Quarterly, [Legal Barriers to the Growth of Health Information Exchange: Boulders or Pebbles?](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5835678/) (open access via PMC) is the comprehensive analysis that places the Cures Act information-blocking provisions in context: what HIPAA already permitted, what state consent law constrains, what 42 CFR Part 2 changed for substance-use disclosure, and what the unfunded national patient identifier still costs. The paper is the standard reference for understanding why the Cures Act looks the way it does.
