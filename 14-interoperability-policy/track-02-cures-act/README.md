# Track 02: 21st Century Cures Act

The 21st Century Cures Act is a 2016 federal law that, alongside its FDA-reform and NIH-funding provisions, contained the statutory move the field had been describing for a decade without a name: it prohibited information blocking and gave the Department of Health and Human Services the authority to define the prohibition operationally. The Cures Act is the load-bearing statute behind every interoperability rule the next several years would produce, and reading it (specifically Section 4004 and the related ONC and OIG implementing rules) is the prerequisite for reading any rule that came after.

Three structural pieces of the Cures Act do the work of this track. Section 4004 contains the statutory definition of information blocking and the cause of action against it. The 2020 ONC Cures Act Final Rule catalogues the exceptions that distinguish blocking from legitimate restriction (eight in the original Final Rule, raised to ten by HTI-1's addition of a TEFCA Manner Exception and the Protecting Care Access Exception in 2024); each exception specifies the conditions under which a practice that would otherwise meet the blocking definition is excepted from the prohibition. The mandate framework requires standardized FHIR R4 APIs and the USCDI baseline data set for certified health IT and for CMS-regulated payers, which is what Track 03 covers in detail.
