# Track 04: The international landscape

A clinician comparing US and EU health-data law often expects the comparison to reduce to HIPAA vs GDPR with the EU being stricter. The reality has more structure. The structural differences are what determine how each system actually operates in practice and what an interoperability policy can or cannot mandate on top of the legal floor. Track 04 covers the GDPR framework as it applies to health data, the UK NHS approach (a single-payer system with a universal patient identifier and a national integration platform), and the European Health Data Space (EHDS), the 2025 EU regulation that creates a common framework for primary and secondary use of health data across member states.

Three structural differences distinguish the EU framework from the US framework. GDPR sets a higher consent floor than HIPAA, because processing of health data (a special category under Article 9) requires both a lawful basis under Article 6 and an Article 9 condition, which is structurally stricter than HIPAA's broad treatment-payment-operations permission. The EHDS regulates primary use (clinical care and patient access) and secondary use (research, public health, regulatory) of health data as separate regimes with different rights, different access mechanisms, and different governance bodies, which the US has not done at federal level. And most EU member states have a national patient identifier (the NHS Number in England and the equivalents in other countries), which removes the patient-matching problem that Track 05 will name as the load-bearing remaining gap in the US framework.
