## Go deeper: Financial management for informaticists

**If you want the canonical worked example of clinical-system ROI:**

- [Kaushal et al., "Return on Investment for a Computerized Physician Order Entry System" (JAMIA, 2006)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1513660/) is the classic published ROI analysis of a clinical information system: ten years of CPOE at Brigham and Women's Hospital, $11.8M in costs against $28.5M in benefits, with break-even arriving after year five. The cost structure (development, then a long maintenance tail) and the benefit structure (decision support categories dominate) are the same structures this track's budget uses, at hospital scale. The longest-lasting lesson is the payback horizon: the institution needed patience measured in years, not quarters.

**If you want the evidence base on whether CDS pays for itself:**

- [Lewkowicz et al., "Economic impact of clinical decision support interventions based on electronic health records" (BMC Health Services Research, 2020)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7491136/) is a free systematic review of 27 economic evaluations of EHR-based CDS: 22 report reduced spending, concentrated in avoided duplicate orders, unnecessary testing, and transfusion practice. Two findings bear directly on this track. Most studies neglect implementation and maintenance costs, the same invisible majority the TCO section names, and some CDS features (order facilitators, malfunctions) raise spending rather than lowering it.

**If you want the vendor-contract side in working depth:**

- [EHR Contracts Untangled: Selecting Wisely, Negotiating Terms, and Understanding the Fine Print (ONC, 2016)](https://www.healthit.gov/sites/default/files/EHR_Contracts_Untangled.pdf) is a free federal guide written for ONC by attorneys who negotiate health-IT contracts, covering pre-selection planning and nine negotiation areas including warranties, data rights, integration, liability, and termination. The maintenance-fee, lock-in, and exit-cost concepts in this track's vendor section are treated there at contract-clause depth. Read the termination and data-access sections before any build-vs-buy recommendation reaches a committee.
