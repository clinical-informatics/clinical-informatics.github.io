## Go deeper: Healthcare quality improvement and operations management

**If you want the canonical improvement-method reference:**

- [How to Improve: Model for Improvement (Institute for Healthcare Improvement)](https://www.ihi.org/resources/how-improve-model-improvement) is the primary source for the method this track applies to the RA-CDS: the three questions, the PDSA cycle, and the practical steps (forming the team, setting aims, establishing measures, selecting and testing changes). The page is free and links onward to IHI's measure and testing worksheets, which are the templates a working QI team actually fills in. Reading the aims and measures sections against the track's worked PDSA shows how little ceremony the method requires when the change is small and the prediction is explicit.

**If you want the statistical grounding for run and control charts:**

- [Benneyan, Lloyd, and Plsek, "Statistical process control as a tool for research and healthcare improvement" (Quality and Safety in Health Care, 2003)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1758030/) is the standard healthcare introduction to SPC, free in full text on PubMed Central. It develops the common-cause vs special-cause distinction, the chart-selection logic (which chart for which data type), and worked clinical examples, at exactly the depth a working informaticist needs to defend a control chart in front of a skeptical committee. The paper also states the case against summary statistics that aggregate away the time order, which is the formal version of this track's anti-tampering claim.

**If you want the measurement-framework landscape from the payer side:**

- [HEDIS and Performance Measurement (NCQA)](https://www.ncqa.org/hedis/) is NCQA's own overview of the measure set behind most payer quality programs: the six domains, the reporting model, and the shift toward measures computed from electronic clinical data. More than 235 million Americans are enrolled in plans that report HEDIS, which is why its data demands reach provider informatics teams that never signed up for it. Reading the overview alongside the CMS-side programs named in the notebook (MIPS, eCQMs, hospital IQR) makes the division of the measurement landscape legible: who holds each measure, who reports it, and whose data capture it ultimately depends on.
