## Go deeper: The working clinical informaticist

**If you want the official definition of the subspecialty's scope:**

- [Clinical Informatics, American Board of Preventive Medicine](https://www.theabpm.org/become-certified/subspecialties/clinical-informatics/) is the certifying board's page for the clinical informatics subspecialty: eligibility pathways, exam administration, and the formal description of what the subspecialty covers. The board's content outline is the closest thing the field has to an official statement of the working informaticist's scope; this course covers the "Leading and Managing Change" domain, which the outline weights at roughly 20%. Track 6 returns to the certification pathway as career orientation.

**If you want the definitive statement of what the chief informatics roles require:**

- [The Chief Clinical Informatics Officer (CCIO): AMIA Task Force Report on CCIO Knowledge, Education, and Skillset Requirements (Kannry et al., Applied Clinical Informatics, 2016)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4817341/) treats the CMIO, CNIO, and their pharmacy and dental counterparts as one role family and specifies the knowledge, education, and skills each requires, with competency tables mapped to training pathways. The report exists because the roles were being filled by job description rather than by defined competency, and institutions got widely different things under the same title. Read it alongside the working-week table in this track: the report says what the role must know, the table says what the role spends its hours on.

**If you want the history of how the subspecialty came to exist:**

- [Clinical informatics board certification: history, current status, and predicted impact on the clinical informatics workforce (Detmer, Munger, Lehmann, Applied Clinical Informatics, 2010)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3631890/) traces the path from informal practice to an ABMS-recognized subspecialty: the core-content definition, the ACGME fellowship requirements, and the certification machinery. The paper documents why the field formalized when it did: the national EHR-adoption push exposed a workforce shortage that informal apprenticeship could not fill. Useful context for every role in this track, since the titles stabilized only after the certification did.
