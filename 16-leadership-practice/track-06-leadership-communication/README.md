# Track 06: Leadership and communication

Next month's Board of Trustees agenda allocates you 10 minutes to present the RA flare-risk alert, three slides maximum. What goes on those slides is a test of everything this track covers: choosing a leadership behavior to match the moment, ordering a message so the decision survives interruption, and negotiating from interests rather than positions. Track 06 covers the four leadership styles (transformational, transactional, servant, situational) and the situational case for switching among them; executive communication through BLUF, the what / so what / now what structure, and the one-page discipline; and interest-based negotiation through a live conflict from the deployment, the rheumatology chief's "we will not have another alert in this clinic."

Three claims are load-bearing. Leadership style is a behavior chosen per team and task, not a personality trait: the same deployment needs transactional management in cutover week, servant leadership with the build team, and transformational framing with the skeptical department. BLUF inverts the ordering clinical training rewards: the ask comes first, so that whatever the clock cuts off, the decision has already been heard. And a negotiation turns on the interest underneath the stated position: naming "do not waste my clinicians' time" beneath "no more alerts" converts *whether* into *under what conditions*. The track's interactive is the pitch itself: a 3-slide builder (the ask, the evidence, the risk) with per-choice feedback and a reactive assembled deck. The artifact, the executive-pitch summary, becomes a section of the capstone's implementation plan.

One section addresses the clinical informatics career path as orientation: AMIA 10x10, the two-year ACGME-accredited fellowship, the ABPM certification exam, and continuing certification. This curriculum maps to much of the board blueprint, and Course 16 addresses the Leading and Managing Change domain directly, but it is not a board-prep product.
