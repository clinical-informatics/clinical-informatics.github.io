## Go deeper: Leadership and communication

**If you want the research case for switching leadership styles:**

- [Leadership That Gets Results, by Daniel Goleman (Harvard Business Review, 2000)](https://hbr.org/2000/03/leadership-that-gets-results) is the classic research treatment of leadership styles: six styles derived from a large study of executives, each measured by the organizational-climate effect it produces. Goleman's taxonomy differs from the four named theories in this track's table, but the central finding is the same situational case: effective leaders switch among styles as the situation demands rather than perfecting one. The landing page and summary are free; the full article sits behind HBR's metered paywall, so use an institutional library if the meter blocks it.

**If you want the certification pathway from the certifying board itself:**

- [Clinical Informatics at the American Board of Preventive Medicine](https://www.theabpm.org/become-certified/subspecialties/clinical-informatics/) is the authoritative page for the subspecialty: the ACGME-accredited fellowship pathway (a minimum of 24 months of accredited training), the application materials and verifications, exam dates and fees, and the Continuing Certification Program. The exam content outline linked from this page is the blueprint this course's Leading and Managing Change coverage maps to. Read it before committing to a fellowship application.

**If you want the evidence for what working informaticists actually do:**

- [Domains, tasks, and knowledge for clinical informatics subspecialty practice: results of a practice analysis (Silverman et al., JAMIA 2019)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7647208/) is the practice analysis behind the current board blueprint: 5 domains, 42 tasks, and 139 knowledge and skill statements, validated by a survey of certified diplomates. It is the empirical answer to "what does this job consist of," and the leadership, change-management, and communication tasks it documents are the ones Course 16 teaches. Free full text on PMC.
