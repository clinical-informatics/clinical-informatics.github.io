## Go deeper: Implementation and operations of clinical information systems

**If you want the safety-practice framework for downtime and contingency planning:**

- [SAFER Guides (ONC)](https://www.healthit.gov/topic/safety/safer-guides) are eight free self-assessment guides on the safe use of EHRs, published by the Office of the National Coordinator and revised in 2025. The Contingency Planning guide is the one this track's downtime section maps to: recommended practices for planned and unplanned EHR unavailability, covering paper workflows, read-only backup access, the recovery sequence, and post-restoration reconciliation. The guides are interactive PDFs designed for an institution to complete as a self-assessment, which makes them usable as an audit instrument for an operations plan.

**If you want the service-management framework underneath the support tiers and the change-control board:**

- [ITIL 4: Guiding Principles and Practices (Atlassian)](https://www.atlassian.com/itsm/itil) is a free overview of ITIL, the IT service-management framework that hospital IT operations borrow their vocabulary from: incident versus problem versus change, service levels, and continual improvement. The tier structure, the SLA, and the standard/normal/emergency change taxonomy in this track are all ITIL practices applied to healthcare examples. The page is vendor-hosted but framework-neutral in its coverage, and it supplies the vocabulary institutional IT operations teams use.

**If you want the research grounding on what downtime does to patient safety:**

- [Implications of electronic health record downtime: an analysis of patient safety event reports (Larsen, Fong, Wernz, Ratwani; JAMIA 2017)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7647128/) analyzed 80,381 safety event reports and identified 76 explicitly tied to EHR downtime, with laboratory processes accounting for nearly half and medication ordering and administration next. In 46% of the downtime-associated events, downtime procedures either were not followed or did not exist; that finding carries the track's argument. The paper makes the case that the downtime plan is a patient-safety control, not an IT formality, and it is free in full text on PMC.
