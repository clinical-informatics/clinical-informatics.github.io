## Go deeper: Change management

**If you want the canonical organization-level framework from its author:**

- [Kotter's 8-step process for leading change (Kotter Inc.)](https://www.kotterinc.com/methodology/8-steps/) is the free overview of the framework this track applies to the RA-CDS, maintained by Kotter's own firm, with a short description of each step. The framework originated as the 1995 Harvard Business Review article ["Leading Change: Why Transformation Efforts Fail"](https://hbr.org/1995/05/leading-change-why-transformation-efforts-fail-2), which remains the sharpest statement of the eight failure modes but sits behind the HBR paywall; the overview page covers the same eight steps without it.

**If you want the implementation account of a real clinical AI deployment:**

- [Sendak et al., "Real-World Integration of a Sepsis Deep Learning Technology Into Routine Clinical Care: Implementation Study" (JMIR Medical Informatics 2020)](https://medinform.jmir.org/2020/7/e15182/) documents what Duke built for Sepsis Watch: the model, the rapid-response-nurse dashboard workflow, the threshold decisions, and the governance review. It is open access under a Creative Commons license, and it is the engineering half of the case study this track pairs with the Elish and Watkins ethnography. Course 12 Track 4 uses the same paper as its CDS-evaluation reference.

**If you want the half the implementation paper could not show:**

- [Elish and Watkins, "Repairing Innovation: A Study of Integrating AI in Clinical Care" (Data & Society 2020)](https://datasociety.net/library/repairing-innovation/) is the ethnography of the same deployment, free as a PDF from the landing page. It names the work of repair: the trust-building, the workflow negotiation, and the nurses' largely invisible integration labor that made a technically sound system function inside a social one. The report is the strongest available argument that a deployment plan is a hypothesis about workflow and that the people inside the workflow finish the design.
