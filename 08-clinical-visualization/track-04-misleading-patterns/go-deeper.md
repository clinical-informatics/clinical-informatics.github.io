# Go deeper: Common misleading patterns

**If you want to understand the patterns better before moving on:**

- [How Charts Lie: Getting Smarter about Visual Information](https://www.albertocairo.com/howchartslie) by Alberto Cairo is the most accessible single source on misleading visualization. The book is organized by the same patterns the track presents (truncation, cherry-picking, dual-axis, inappropriate chart types), with extensive published examples. Cairo's [website](https://www.albertocairo.com/) hosts free essays that extend the book's material.
- [Fundamentals of Data Visualization](https://clauswilke.com/dataviz/), Chapter 17 "Proportional ink," and Chapter 25 "The principle of proportional ink and chart deception" specify the rule against truncated axes on bar charts and explain why the rule is weaker for line charts.

**If you want to see this applied clinically:**

- The [BMJ Statistics Notes series](https://www.bmj.com/specialties/statistics-notes) by Altman and Bland is the canonical clinical reference on chart-based misrepresentation. The notes on "Variables and parameters," "Quartiles, quintiles, centiles," and on the visual display of survival curves cover the clinical-research equivalents of the patterns the track presents.
- The [EQUATOR Network](https://www.equator-network.org/) hosts the published reporting guidelines (CONSORT, STROBE, TRIPOD, and others). Each guideline has a section on figures and tables that specifies the chart-construction expectations for that study type. The guidelines are the published consensus on what is and is not acceptable in a clinical chart.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [Calling Bullshit: The Art of Skepticism in a Data-Driven World](https://www.callingbullshit.org/) by Carl Bergstrom and Jevin West is a free companion to the University of Washington course of the same name. The course site hosts open lecture videos and reading lists. Chapter 7 "Data visualization" and the associated lecture (chapter title "Misleading axes on graphs") present the same patterns the track demonstrates, with examples drawn from political and scientific publications.
