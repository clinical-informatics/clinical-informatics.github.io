# Go deeper: Choosing the right chart type

**If you want to understand the data-type-to-chart-type decision better before moving on:**

- [Fundamentals of Data Visualization](https://clauswilke.com/dataviz/) by Claus Wilke is free online. The first three sections of the table of contents ("Visualizing data", "Visualizing distributions", "Visualizing associations") align with the decision the track presents. Read Chapter 5 "Directory of visualizations" for a comprehensive catalogue keyed by the question the chart answers.
- The [Data Visualisation Catalogue](https://datavizcatalogue.com/) is a free reference that lists chart types and the data shape each one fits. The "What's the function?" filter on the home page is the same decision the track frames as "data type to chart type."

**If you want to see this applied clinically:**

- The [Financial Times Visual Vocabulary](https://github.com/Financial-Times/chart-doctor) is the most widely cited single-page reference for matching a chart type to a question. The PDF poster classifies charts under nine intentions (deviation, correlation, ranking, distribution, change over time, magnitude, part-to-whole, spatial, flow); it is the same decision tree applied at scale to news graphics.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [The Visual Display of Quantitative Information](https://www.edwardtufte.com/book/the-visual-display-of-quantitative-information/) by Edward Tufte is the canonical reference. The chapters on graphical excellence and chartjunk specify the criteria by which a chart is judged. Tufte's argument that the chart's visual content should be dominated by the data, not by the chart's decoration, is the philosophical source of most of the recommendations in this track.
