# Go deeper: Visualizing uncertainty

**If you want to understand the three uncertainty objects better before moving on:**

- [Fundamentals of Data Visualization](https://clauswilke.com/dataviz/), Chapter 16 "Visualizing uncertainty," is the most accessible single source. The chapter distinguishes the standard error, the confidence interval, and the prediction interval explicitly and shows the chart each produces. Wilke's "frequency framing" alternative to abstract probability bands is also worth reading.
- The [American Statistical Association statement on p-values](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf) (Wasserstein and Lazar 2016) is the canonical reference on what a p-value does and does not communicate. The same logic governs what a confidence interval does and does not communicate, and the statement is the most commonly cited starting point.

**If you want to see this applied clinically:**

- The [BMJ Statistics Notes on confidence intervals](https://www.bmj.com/specialties/statistics-notes) (Altman and Bland) are free and short. The note "Statistics with confidence" makes the clinical case that the confidence interval is more useful than the point estimate or the p-value for decisions a clinician has to make. The "Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence" note in the same series is the most cited clinical illustration of the difference between a wide interval that includes zero and a true null effect.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [The Art of Statistics: How to Learn from Data](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/575546/the-art-of-statistics-by-david-spiegelhalter/) by David Spiegelhalter is the most complete general-audience treatment of statistical communication, including chart-based uncertainty. Chapter 9 "Putting probability and statistics together" presents the same three uncertainty objects the track defines and shows the clinical and policy examples in which each one is the right choice. Spiegelhalter's [Winton Centre for Risk and Evidence Communication](https://wintoncentre.maths.cam.ac.uk/) website hosts free working papers on visual risk communication that extend the book's material.
