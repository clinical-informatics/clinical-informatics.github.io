## Go deeper

**If you want to understand the field's professional structure before moving on:**
- The American Medical Informatics Association's main site, particularly the *Clinical Informatics* section under the careers menu. Describes the working roles named in Track 06, the AMIA 10x10 program, the master's-and-fellowship pathways, and the ABPM board certification. AMIA is the field's primary professional society and the page is the closest thing the field has to a single canonical orientation. [amia.org](https://amia.org)

**If you want to see the literature the track named in its native habitat:**
- The current issue of the *Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association* (JAMIA). Open the table of contents and read three abstracts that catch your eye. The goal is to get a sense for what *the literature of the field* actually looks like: the methods, the framings, the typical study size, the kinds of authors. Pair with the *AMIA Annual Symposium proceedings* index on PubMed Central, which is free and shows the same year's most recent applied work. [academic.oup.com/jamia](https://academic.oup.com/jamia), [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) (search *AMIA Annual Symposium proceedings*).

**If you want to go significantly further toward formal credentialing:**
- The American Board of Preventive Medicine's *Clinical Informatics* subspecialty page. The official pathway documentation for the board exam: eligibility (an ABMS primary specialty, the practice or fellowship pathway), the content outline, the maintenance-of-certification requirements. For physicians who want to combine board certification with the credentials the field is converging on, this is the road map. The page is the authoritative source for the current rules; they change occasionally as the subspecialty matures. [theabpm.org](https://theabpm.org) (look for *Clinical Informatics* under *Become Certified*).
