## Go deeper

**If you want a thoughtful working definition of the field before moving on:**
- Edward Shortliffe and Marsden Blois, *The Computer Meets Medicine and Biology: Emergence of a Discipline*. Chapter 1 of the canonical Shortliffe textbook *Biomedical Informatics* (now in its 5th edition, 2020). The chapter is the field's most cited working definition of itself and is what most informatics fellowships hand out in the first week. The full textbook is not free, but the chapter is widely available through university libraries. As a free alternative covering similar ground, AMIA's working definitions and field-overview material lives at the organization's site: [amia.org](https://amia.org). Look in particular for the *Why Informatics* and *Clinical Informatics* sections.

**If you want to see how the historical moments from this track played out in one document:**
- The 21st Century Cures Act Final Rule, published by the Office of the National Coordinator for Health IT (ONC). The rule's preamble walks the FHIR adoption story (Track 01's fourth moment) and the information-blocking provisions that followed. Reads as a worked example of what happens when one of the historical inflection points actually lands as enforceable policy. [healthit.gov/topic/oncs-cures-act-final-rule](https://www.healthit.gov/topic/oncs-cures-act-final-rule)

**If you want to go significantly further:**
- AMIA's 10x10 program. The widely-taken introductory program for clinicians and others entering the field, developed by AMIA in partnership with universities. About one semester in length, online, structured for working clinicians. The most common formal first step for people who want to move from *learned on the job* toward *formally trained*. Course catalog and enrollment at [amia.org](https://amia.org). Look for the *10x10* page under education.
