# Track 01: What clinical informatics is and how it got here

Clinical informatics is the field that studies and shapes how clinical information is captured, stored, moved, surfaced, and used in the practice of healthcare. That definition sits between two phrases that do most of the work: *the practice of healthcare* (the field is anchored to actual patient care, not to abstract information theory) and *captured, stored, moved, surfaced, and used* (the lifecycle of clinical data that Track 02 names formally).

This track puts that definition to work. The first move is a worked example: a rheumatology clinic morning, six tools on one screen, each one the visible tip of a piece of informatics work. The second is the short history that produced what the chart looks like today, organized around five moments that did most of the explanatory work.

1. **Lawrence Weed and the POMR (1960s).** The first time someone argued that the *structure* of the medical record was a clinical intervention. The reason every chart has a problem list at the front.
2. **The NLM buildout (1970s through 1990s).** Don Lindberg and the National Library of Medicine made medical knowledge machine-readable and clinical vocabularies interoperable. The reason your EHR can map a local lab code to LOINC.
3. **HITECH and Meaningful Use (2009).** Roughly $30 billion of federal money put EHRs in nearly every U.S. hospital within five years. The reason clinical informatics is a job title in most health systems now.
4. **HL7 FHIR (2010s).** Clinical data borrowed the architecture of the web. The reason a third-party tool can integrate with an EHR in two weeks instead of two years.
5. **The 2020s AI inflection.** Ambient documentation and embedded predictive models graduated from research papers to vendor products. The field's current attention.

The track closes by formally introducing **Ms. Elena Reyes**, the synthetic 52-year-old with seropositive rheumatoid arthritis who appears in every course of the curriculum. Her files live in `start-here/patients/elena-reyes/` and are accessible from inside every course through the `patients/` symlink.
