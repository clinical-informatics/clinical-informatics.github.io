## Go deeper

**If you want to understand this better before moving on:**
- Boston University School of Public Health, "Measures of Disease Frequency" and "Measures of Association" modules in their open EP713 textbook. The clearest free written explanation of incidence, prevalence, RR, OR, and HR I have found, with worked clinical examples. Pair the two modules and you have a self-contained reference you can come back to. [sphweb.bumc.bu.edu/otlt/MPH-Modules/EP/EP713_Association/](https://sphweb.bumc.bu.edu/otlt/MPH-Modules/EP/EP713_Association/)

**If you want to see this applied clinically:**
- Galloway, Hyrich, Mercer, Dixon, Fu, Ustianowski, Watson, Lunt, Symmons, and the BSRBR Control Centre Consortium, "Anti-TNF therapy is associated with an increased risk of serious infections in patients with rheumatoid arthritis especially in the first 6 months of treatment: updated results from the British Society for Rheumatology Biologics Register with special emphasis on risks in the elderly." *Rheumatology*, 2011, 50(1):124-131. Free on PMC. Real-world cohort study our synthetic cohort is loosely modeled on. 11,798 anti-TNF and 3,598 csDMARD patients; adjusted HR 1.2 (95% CI 1.1, 1.5) for serious infection, with risk concentrated in the first six months. Reports incidence *rates* per 1000 person-years rather than collapsing to a single 3-year RR, which is exactly the case for using rates and HRs when follow-up is unequal. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3105607/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3105607/)

**If you want to go significantly further:**
- Rothman, Greenland, and Lash, *Modern Epidemiology*, 3rd edition (Lippincott). Chapters 3 and 4 are the canonical treatment of measures of frequency and effect, including the math that justifies the rare-disease approximation, the relationship between cumulative incidence and incidence density, and the case for incidence-density sampling in case-control design. Not free, but every academic medical library has it. The free alternative that overlaps about 60% of this material is Pearce, *A Short Introduction to Epidemiology*, 2nd edition, available openly at [openaccess.wgtn.ac.nz/articles/book/A_short_introduction_to_epidemiology/12996386](https://openaccess.wgtn.ac.nz/articles/book/A_short_introduction_to_epidemiology/12996386).
