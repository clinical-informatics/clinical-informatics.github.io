# Go deeper: What CDS actually is

**If you want to understand the CDS framework better before moving on:**

- [Osheroff et al., "Improving Outcomes with Clinical Decision Support: An Implementer's Guide" (HIMSS, 2nd ed)](https://www.himss.org/) is the canonical reference work that defined the five-rights framework. The five-rights chapter is the standard introduction to designed-vs-noisy CDS and is the document most CDS implementation papers cite.
- [The CDS Hooks documentation](https://cds-hooks.org/) covers the modern standards-based delivery architecture (addressed in detail in Track 03). The site's "Why CDS Hooks" overview frames the historical problem the standard solves.

**If you want to see the alert-fatigue evidence:**

- [van der Sijs et al., "Overriding of drug safety alerts in computerized physician order entry" (JAMIA 2006)](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/13/2/138/734105) is the load-bearing override-rate reference. The paper established the 49 to 96 percent override range that subsequent CDS literature has repeatedly confirmed.
- [Khairat et al., "Reasons For Physicians Not Adopting Clinical Decision Support Systems: Critical Analysis" (JMIR Med Inform 2018)](https://medinform.jmir.org/2018/2/e24/) is a free, open-access systematic review of the operational reasons CDS gets abandoned.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [Sittig and Singh, "A new sociotechnical model for studying health information technology in complex adaptive healthcare systems" (Quality and Safety in Health Care 2010)](https://qualitysafety.bmj.com/content/19/Suppl_3/i68) is the eight-dimensional sociotechnical model that frames CDS as a sociotechnical artifact rather than a software artifact. The eight dimensions (hardware/software, clinical content, human-computer interface, people, workflow/communication, organizational policies, external rules, system measurement) are the unit of analysis behind most modern CDS-failure-mode literature.
