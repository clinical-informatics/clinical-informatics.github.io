# Go deeper: Evaluating CDS

**If you want to understand the CDS evaluation framework better before moving on:**

- [Bright et al., "Effect of clinical decision-support systems: a systematic review" (Annals of Internal Medicine 2012)](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/0003-4819-157-1-201207030-00450) is the AHRQ-commissioned systematic review that established the evidence base for CDS evaluation. The methods section is a useful reference for what counts as an acceptable CDS evaluation design.
- [Kawamoto et al., "Improving clinical practice using clinical decision support systems: a systematic review of trials to identify features critical to success" (BMJ 2005)](https://www.bmj.com/content/330/7494/765) identified the four success factors (automatic provision in workflow, recommendations not just assessments, point-of-care delivery, computer-based generation) that most subsequent CDS evaluation frameworks reference.

**If you want to see this applied clinically:**

- [Sendak et al., "Real-World Integration of a Sepsis Deep Learning Technology Into Routine Clinical Care: Implementation Study" (JMIR Med Inform 2020)](https://medinform.jmir.org/2020/7/e15182/) is the Duke Sepsis Watch implementation paper (cited in Course 09 Track 4). It is also a canonical reference for CDS evaluation done right: the paper documents the workflow integration, the alert design, the threshold selection, the prospective evaluation, and the governance review.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [Vickers and Elkin, "Decision curve analysis: a novel method for evaluating prediction models" (Medical Decision Making 2006)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0272989X06295361) is the foundational DCA paper (cited in Course 11 Track 5). The application to CDS alert-threshold selection is the operational form the track presents.
- [Wagner et al., "Segmented regression analysis of interrupted time series studies in medication use research" (Journal of Clinical Pharmacy and Therapeutics 2002)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1046/j.1365-2710.2002.00430.x) is the standard methods reference for the segmented-regression analysis of an interrupted time series, which is the most common before-after design for CDS evaluation.
