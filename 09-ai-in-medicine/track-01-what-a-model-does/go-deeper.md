# Go deeper: What a model actually does

**If you want to understand the function-fitting intuition better before moving on:**

- [StatQuest with Josh Starmer](https://statquest.org/) is the most accessible free resource for the intuition behind statistical learning. The introductory playlists on linear regression and on neural networks cover the same idea the track presents (a model is a function whose parameters are fit to data) in 10-to-15-minute videos with clinical-style examples.
- [3Blue1Brown's neural network series](https://www.3blue1brown.com/topics/neural-networks) is the canonical free introduction to how a neural network maps inputs to outputs. The first three episodes are the load-bearing ones; they explain the function-fitting picture geometrically without requiring matrix algebra.

**If you want to see this applied clinically:**

- [The MIT Critical Care Database (MIMIC)](https://physionet.org/content/mimiciv/) is the most widely used free clinical dataset for AI research. Reading two or three papers built on MIMIC alongside the dataset documentation is the fastest way to see what "training a model on clinical data" actually involves at the pipeline level.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [An Introduction to Statistical Learning](https://www.statlearning.com/) by James, Witten, Hastie, and Tibshirani is free in both R and Python editions and is the standard graduate text. Chapter 2 ("Statistical Learning") and Chapter 3 ("Linear Regression") cover the function-fitting framing of the track at the level a clinical researcher would need to read methods sections.
