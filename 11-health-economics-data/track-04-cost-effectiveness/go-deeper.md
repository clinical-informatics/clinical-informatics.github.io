# Go deeper: Cost-effectiveness

**If you want to understand the cost-effectiveness framework better before moving on:**

- [The CHEERS reporting checklist](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/cheers/) (Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards) is the published reporting standard for any cost-effectiveness analysis. The checklist itself is the operational appraisal framework for reading published CE papers.
- [The Cost-Effectiveness Analysis Registry at the Tufts CEVR](https://cevr.tuftsmedicalcenter.org/databases/cea-registry) is the searchable database of published cost-utility analyses. The registry's published-CEA viewer is the fastest way to find the published ICER for a clinical question.

**If you want to see this applied clinically:**

- [The Second Panel on Cost-Effectiveness in Health and Medicine (Sanders et al., JAMA 2016)](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2552214) is the US-consensus methods document for clinical cost-effectiveness analysis. The paper documents the reference-case methods (perspective, discounting, time horizon, willingness-to-pay) that any US-context CE study should follow.
- [The Institute for Clinical and Economic Review (ICER)](https://icer.org/) is the most-cited US value-assessment organization. ICER's published value-assessment reports apply CEA to specific clinical decisions (a new drug, a new device); reading one report is the fastest way to see CEA in action on a current clinical question.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [Drummond et al. "Methods for the Economic Evaluation of Health Care Programmes" (Oxford, 4th ed)](https://global.oup.com/) is the graduate-level reference textbook for cost-effectiveness analysis. It is the standard reference cited in most health-economics methods papers.
