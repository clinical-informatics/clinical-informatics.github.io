# Go deeper: How claims data is structured

**If you want to understand the claims framework better before moving on:**

- [The Resource Information Center (RIC) at the Research Data Assistance Center (ResDAC)](https://resdac.org/) is the canonical reference for US administrative claims data. ResDAC publishes data dictionaries for Medicare, Medicaid, and MA claims that document every field in operational detail. The "Getting Started" pages are the practical entry point.
- The [CMS Claims Data Snapshot](https://www.cms.gov/data-research) (the public-use file collections) gives a feel for the size and shape of national claims datasets. Browsing one or two PUFs alongside ResDAC's data dictionaries clarifies what a "claim" is in operational form.

**If you want to see this applied clinically:**

- [The Hilltop Institute Claims Data Primer (UMBC)](https://www.hilltopinstitute.org/) hosts a clinically-oriented introduction to Medicaid claims data analysis. The primer documents the common claim types (institutional, professional, pharmacy, dental) and the relationships between them at the analytic level.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [The Sentinel System common data model documentation](https://www.sentinelinitiative.org/) is the FDA-led national distributed claims database used for post-market drug surveillance. Reading the Sentinel CDM specification alongside the OMOP CDM specification (Course 07) makes the structural choices that distinguish claims-first vs EHR-first data models explicit and is the right deep reference for an informaticist designing claims-based research.
