# Go deeper: Reading outcomes data critically

**If you want to understand the value-based-care framework better before moving on:**

- [The CMS Innovation Center](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/) maintains the public documentation for the federal value-based-care programs (ACO REACH, Medicare Shared Savings, bundled payments, primary-care first). The model overview pages are the operational reference for what each program measures and how it pays.
- [The Health Care Payment Learning and Action Network (HCP-LAN)](https://hcp-lan.org/) hosts the four-category framework (fee-for-service, FFS with quality, APM built on FFS, population-based) used to classify payment models. The annual measurement report is the standard reference for the national mix of payment models.

**If you want to see this applied clinically:**

- The [STROBE statement](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/strobe/) and the [RECORD-PE extension for studies of medication effects in routinely collected data](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/record/) cover the appraisal of observational outcomes research generally and pharmacoepidemiologic studies in routinely-collected data specifically. The published guidelines are the operational checklist for reading an outcomes paper.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [Schneeweiss "Sensitivity analysis and external adjustment for unmeasured confounders in epidemiologic database studies of therapeutics" (Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety 2006)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/10991557) is the methodological reference for what to do about unmeasured confounding in routinely-collected outcomes data. The paper covers the quantitative tools (sensitivity analysis, external adjustment, instrumental variables) that distinguish a defensible observational study from a study that should not have been published.
