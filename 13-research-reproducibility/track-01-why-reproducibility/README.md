# Track 01: Why reproducibility matters

A landmark preclinical cancer paper is cited a thousand times. A pharmaceutical team spends two years and several million dollars trying to build on it. The result will not come back. When they ask the original lab for the data and the analysis steps, what arrives is a figure, a methods paragraph, and no way to rerun anything. This is the ordinary form of the problem, and it is expensive.

The track defines the three words that the word "reproducibility" is usually doing the work of three separate ideas at once: **reproducible** (same data, same code, same result), **replicable** (new data, same protocol, same conclusion), and **robust** (same data, different defensible analytic choices, same conclusion). It presents the replication crisis with the documented numbers (Begley and Ellis on preclinical cancer, the Open Science Collaboration on psychology, Ioannidis on why the base rate of true findings is lower than the literature implies), and it makes the load-bearing point that an analysis can be fully reproducible and still wrong: the code can faithfully reproduce a biased estimate. Reproducibility is a necessary property, not a sufficient one. The remaining tracks teach the practices that produce it.
