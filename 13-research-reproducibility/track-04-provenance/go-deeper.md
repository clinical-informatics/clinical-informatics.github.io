## Go deeper: Data provenance and documentation

**If you want to understand documentation practice before moving on:**

- [The Turing Way, "Reproducible Research"](https://book.the-turing-way.org/reproducible-research/reproducible-research.html) covers research data management, documentation, and the recording of analytic steps as part of a reproducible workflow. Its data-management material is the plain-language reference for the data-dictionary and transformation-log practices this track introduces.

**If you want to see provenance applied to clinical data:**

- [The Book of OHDSI, Chapter 6 (Extract Transform Load)](https://ohdsi.github.io/TheBookOfOhdsi/) documents the ETL process that maps a source EHR into the OMOP Common Data Model. It is the concrete clinical form of this track's argument and the direct continuation of the Course 07 OMOP work: every code mapping, every convention, and every quality check is a provenance decision, and the chapter shows how a serious project records them. Free under a Creative Commons Zero license.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [The W3C PROV Primer](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-primer/) is the accessible introduction to PROV, the web standard for representing provenance as entities, activities, and agents. It is the formal model behind "how did this number get here," and it is the reference for anyone who needs provenance to be machine-readable rather than only written in a README.
