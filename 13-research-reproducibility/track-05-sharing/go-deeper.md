## Go deeper: Sharing and publication

**If you want the reporting guidelines in one place before moving on:**

- [The EQUATOR Network](https://www.equator-network.org/) is the searchable library of reporting guidelines for health research. It is where an author finds the guideline that fits a given study design (CONSORT for randomized trials, STROBE for observational studies, TRIPOD for prediction models, PRISMA for systematic reviews, STARD for diagnostic-accuracy studies) and the checklist that goes with it. This is the resource to open at the start of writing, not the end.

**If you want the data-stewardship framework:**

- [The FAIR Principles (GO FAIR)](https://www.go-fair.org/fair-principles/) is the open explainer for Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable data. It is the framework most funders and journals now point to for a data-management plan, and it makes the useful distinction this track draws: FAIR is about good stewardship and machine-actionability, which is not the same as making data open.

**If you want to go significantly further:**

- [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/), operated by Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory with BMJ and Yale, is the preprint server for the health sciences. Reading its submission guidance and screening process shows what posting a preprint actually involves: the time-stamped citable record, the "not yet peer reviewed" labeling, and the screening that distinguishes a preprint server from an open-comment website.
