# Track 04: Presenting visuals to non-technical audiences

A trajectory chart that an analyst reads instantly (mean CRP across the cohort over time, with the intervention timepoint annotated and the reference range banded) often goes unread by a non-technical audience presented with it for the first time. The audience does not know what they are looking at; the chart does not say. Track 04 covers the practice of presenting a chart to an audience that has not seen one like it before, the small set of annotations that distinguishes a chart-as-evidence from a chart-as-rhetoric, and the discipline of removing every element that does not serve the specific message the chart is being used to make. The track is the applied form of the visualization principles Course 08 covered.

Three properties of chart presentation are load-bearing for the practice. **The chart must answer a stated question.** The presenter names the question the chart is going to answer (a single sentence) before the chart is shown; the audience reads the chart to verify the answer. **The annotation must serve the question.** The reference range, the intervention timepoint, the period of interest, and the value-at-a-point are added when they help the audience verify the answer; the legend, the gridlines, and the secondary axes are removed when they do not. **The chart has one message.** A chart presented to a non-technical audience is a chart-as-rhetoric (a chart in service of a specific claim); the analyst's instinct to present "the data" and let the audience interpret is the default failure mode, because the audience does not have the analyst's framing.
