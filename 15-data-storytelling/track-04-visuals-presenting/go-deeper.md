## Go deeper: Presenting visuals to non-technical audiences

**If you want a working guide to chart-making for non-technical audiences:**

- [The Datawrapper blog](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/) is the working chart-making resource for journalists, communicators, and anyone presenting data to non-technical readers. The site's posts include "A friendly guide to choosing a chart type" and the Data-Vis Do's and Don'ts series; the editorial perspective is consistently that the chart serves the reader, not the analyst. Datawrapper's tool is widely used; the blog is the conceptual companion that explains why the defaults are what they are.

**If you want the canonical archive of essays on data visualization:**

- [Stephen Few's Perceptual Edge](https://www.perceptualedge.com/) (archived 2003 to 2017) hosts the most substantive long-form treatment of data visualization for communication produced by any single author in the period. The essays specifically on chart choice and dashboard design are the foundational reading; the section on the small-multiples pattern (which Course 08 introduced) and on visual perception are the deeper material. Few's work is the conceptual bridge from Course 08's visualization principles to the presentation discipline this track teaches.

**If you want a library of worked examples for chart-and-slide makeovers:**

- [Storytelling with Data's graph and slide makeover series](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/) is the highest-quality worked-example library in the field. Each makeover walks an analyst's first-draft chart through the specific revisions that turn it from a chart-as-evidence into a chart-as-rhetoric for a stated audience and message. The makeover format makes the craft moves visible in a way that direct instruction usually cannot. The site's free content includes the blog and the YouTube channel.
