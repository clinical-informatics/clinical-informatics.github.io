## Go deeper: Building a narrative

**If you want the canonical narrative-craft framework:**

- [Made to Stick by Chip and Dan Heath](https://heathbrothers.com/books/made-to-stick/) presents the six-principle SUCCES framework (Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories) that defines what distinguishes ideas that survive contact with an audience from ideas that do not. Each principle is the basis for one or more craft moves the finding-implication-recommendation structure of this track depends on. The site links to free supplementary materials.

**If you want the data-narrative practice specifically:**

- [Storytelling with Data, by Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/) covers the same three-part structure (finding to implication to recommendation) in the data-communication context. The site's graph-and-slide makeover series is the worked-example library for this track: an analyst's first-draft chart with the data unstated and the implications unnamed, then the revised version with the structure made explicit. The makeover format is the most efficient teaching artifact in the data-communication field.

**If you want to go significantly further into narrative in medicine:**

- Charon, Hermann, and Devlin's 2016 Academic Medicine paper [Close Reading and Creative Writing in Clinical Education](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4721945/) (open access via PMC) is the canonical academic-medicine reference for narrative practice. Charon's framework distinguishes attention, representation, and affiliation as the three operational moves of narrative work; the framework applies directly to communicating clinical-informatics work to clinical audiences, because the work itself is built on attentive reading of clinical data and the communication is the representation step. The Columbia Program in Narrative Medicine is the institutional home of this field.
