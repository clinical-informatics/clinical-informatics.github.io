# Track 01: Knowing your audience

A clinical informaticist has just spent three weeks finalizing the rollout plan for a sepsis CDS intervention. Within the next week she has to brief four very different groups: the IT team that will build the integration, the medical-and-nursing service that will see the alerts, the C-suite signing off on the budget, and (via a patient-and-family advisory council) patients who will be subject to the alert in care. The default mistake is to compose one set of slides and deliver them four times. The right move is to compose four versions of the same set of facts, each shaped for what its audience can act on. Track 01 names the four audiences operationally distinct in clinical informatics, the questions each one will ask, and the practice of identifying the audience before composing the message.

Four audiences are load-bearing for the rest of the course. **Technical** audiences (engineers, data scientists, analysts) want precision, complete specifications, testability, and failure modes; they read for the things that determine whether they can build what is being described. **Clinical** audiences (clinicians, nurses, pharmacists) want workflow fit, alert behavior at the bedside, the override mechanism, and the harm-reduction case; they read for the things that determine whether the intervention will be safe and usable. **Executive** audiences (CMIO, CMO, CIO, CFO) want the KPI move, the dollar number, the risk, and the time horizon; they read for the things that determine whether to approve, fund, or defer. **Patient** audiences want the experience, the consent context, the safety implications, and the recourse pathway; they read for the things that determine whether they trust the intervention applied to themselves or their family.
