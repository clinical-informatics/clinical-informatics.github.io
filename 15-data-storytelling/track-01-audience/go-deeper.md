## Go deeper: Knowing your audience

**If you want a single accessible foundational reference:**

- [Storytelling with Data, by Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/) is the working data-communication practitioner's website. The blog, podcast, graph-makeover series, and learning videos are free; the worked examples organize themselves around the four audience types this track names. The site's "audience" content is the practical reference for the move from "I have this finding" to "I have this finding to communicate to this person." The book of the same name is the longer-form reference.

**If you want the research grounding for audience analysis in professional writing:**

- [Writing for Busy Readers, by Todd Rogers and Jessica Lasky-Fink](https://writingforbusyreaders.com/) (Harvard) presents six research-grounded principles for writing that actually gets read by professional readers. The book's argument is that almost all professional writing fails because the writer does not work backward from what the busy reader will see in the first five seconds. The companion website hosts the AI prompt and other resources; the book is the longer-form reference.

**If you want the canonical communication-craft framework:**

- [Made to Stick, by Chip and Dan Heath](https://heathbrothers.com/books/made-to-stick/) is the canonical short book on what makes a message memorable, organized around the six-principle SUCCES framework (Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories). The audience-targeting case for each principle is the connecting thread. The site links to free supplementary materials including the elevator-pitch tips and an opening chapter.
