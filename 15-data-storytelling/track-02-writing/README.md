# Track 02: Writing about data clearly

A draft sentence reads: "Multivariable logistic-regression analysis controlling for age, sex, comorbidity index, and prior admissions demonstrated a statistically significant association between the implementation of the sepsis decision support intervention and 30-day in-hospital mortality (adjusted odds ratio 0.78, 95% confidence interval 0.62 to 0.97, p = 0.024)." It is technically correct and difficult to act on. Track 02 covers the craft of writing the same finding in language a non-statistician executive or non-statistician clinician can use, without surrendering the precision the underlying analysis requires. The track presents plain English as a register, the practice of explaining uncertainty without burying the finding, and the recurring phrases that obscure rather than communicate.

Three properties of clear data writing are load-bearing for the rest of the course. The headline number must be the substantive finding (the change a reader could act on), not the test statistic or the analytic procedure. Uncertainty is reported in language the reader can hold (a range, a worst-case, or a comparison to a meaningful threshold), not in language that requires the reader to interpret a confidence interval. Throat-clearing (the "it is important to note that," the "what I want to say," the "one thing worth pointing out") is deleted so the substantive claim leads, not the writer's stance toward the claim.
