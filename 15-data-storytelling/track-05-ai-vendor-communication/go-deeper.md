## Go deeper: Communicating with AI teams and vendors

**If you want the industry framework for evaluating clinical AI:**

- [The Coalition for Health AI (CHAI)](https://chai.org/) is the U.S.-based industry-and-academic coalition that produces frameworks for responsible AI in healthcare. CHAI's Responsible AI Guide, the Applied Model Card specification, and the public registry of clinical AI applications are the working references for what a vendor should be disclosing and what a buyer should be asking. CHAI is the practical complement to the regulatory framework (FDA SaMD, ONC HTI-1 DSI requirements) that governs the same products.

**If you want the reporting standard that defines the disclosures:**

- [The TRIPOD statement](https://www.tripod-statement.org/) is the EQUATOR-network reporting guideline for prediction models, including the 2024 TRIPOD+AI extension for AI/ML models. The 27-item TRIPOD+AI checklist names what a vendor's published validation should report: source of data, predictors, outcome, sample size, training population, performance characteristics including discrimination and calibration, and subgroup performance. The TRIPOD+AI checklist is the operational basis for the question "does the vendor disclose the things a reasonable buyer would need to know," which is the question Course 09's evaluation framework built toward.

**If you want the regulatory frame for clinical AI as a medical device:**

- [The FDA's Artificial Intelligence Enabled Medical Devices page](http://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/software-medical-device-samd/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices) is the federal regulator's working list of FDA-cleared AI-enabled medical devices, with the cleared indications and the date of clearance for each. The page is the buyer-side check for whether a vendor's claims align with what the device is actually cleared for, and the FDA's accompanying guidance documents on AI/ML SaMD lifecycle management are the regulatory backdrop for the HTI-1 DSI requirements covered in Course 14.
